# Interview Question Bank

Use these prompts selectively. Do not dump the full questionnaire on the user. Ask one dimension at a time, reflect the answer, and skip dimensions already established.

## 1. Role and mission

**Question:** What should this agent's central role be in your work or life?

Useful options:
- Executive and operational right hand
- Strategic adviser and challenger
- Technical specialist
- Balanced generalist

**Synthesis:** Convert the answer into an outcome-oriented mission, not a job-title list.

## 2. Autonomy

**Question:** How much initiative should the agent take?

Useful options:
- Wait for explicit instructions
- Recommend, then wait
- Act on safe tasks; confirm sensitive actions
- Highly autonomous; interrupt only for material risk

**Adaptive answer synthesis:**
- Low risk + reversible + clear intent → advance.
- Ambiguous + consequential → present options.
- Destructive, irreversible, external, or sensitive → confirm scope.
- Material risk → stop and propose a safer path.

## 3. Tone

**Question:** What should the everyday personality feel like?

Axes to clarify:
- direct ↔ diplomatic
- serious ↔ playful
- calm ↔ energetic
- supportive ↔ demanding
- formal ↔ conversational

Translate adjectives into behavior. Example: "direct and demanding" means challenge weak assumptions and give an executable alternative, not insult people.

## 4. Disagreement

**Question:** What should happen when the agent believes a decision is weak, incoherent, or risky?

Useful options:
- Follow without challenge
- Warn once, then follow
- Challenge firmly and propose a better option
- Refuse whenever it disagrees

Distinguish ordinary disagreement from safety or authorization boundaries.

## 5. Stakeholders

**Question:** Whom does the agent primarily serve?

Patterns:
- One principal user
- An organization and its authorized members
- A public/community audience
- Context-dependent hierarchy

Clarify authorization, conflicts of interest, and whether members are treated equally.

## 6. Value priority

**Question:** When values conflict, which comes first?

Common priorities:
- Safety and data protection
- Truth and transparency
- Speed and results
- Quality and precision

The selected first principle should shape trade-offs throughout the identity.

## 7. Response depth

**Question:** How detailed should normal responses be?

If the answer is "it depends," define the rule:
- simple/low-impact → short;
- technical/high-impact → evidence and detail;
- user-requested format overrides the default.

## 8. Unacceptable behavior

Use an open question:

> What behaviors would irritate you or be unacceptable?

Examples to prompt recall, not prescribe answers:
- inaccurate information;
- wasted time;
- empty agreement;
- repeated context;
- unnecessary questions;
- fabricated execution results;
- artificial corporate language.

Turn the answer into concrete quality controls.

## 9. Speed versus confidence

**Question:** When speed and certainty conflict, what is the desired standard?

Strong synthesis examples:
- Investigate enough to make a practical recommendation.
- Match verification depth to decision impact.
- Label unresolved uncertainty instead of filling gaps.

## 10. Trust and confidentiality

**Question:** How should internal information be handled among authorized people?

Even with internal trust, credentials, tokens, private keys, personal data, and production secrets should remain need-to-know and should never be echoed gratuitously.

## 11. Errors

**Question:** What should the agent do after making a mistake?

Possible behaviors:
- Correct quietly
- Admit, correct, and explain impact
- Root-cause and prevention analysis
- Detail proportional to severity

A robust error contract includes admission, containment, correction, cause, prevention, and verification.

## 12. Human character

**Question:** How much warmth, humor, competition, or motivational energy should appear?

Guardrails:
- energy without noise;
- competition without arrogance;
- warmth without fake intimacy;
- humor without trivializing risk.

## 13. Metaphor and motto

Ask which image best describes the relationship:
- executor who removes work;
- digital chief of staff;
- copilot who thinks, challenges, and executes;
- operational guardian.

Then ask for mottos, principles, or personal traits to reflect.

## Draft review checklist

Before presenting the draft, verify:

- Does it stand alone as a primary identity?
- Does it define behavior under tension, not merely adjectives?
- Are stakeholders and confidentiality coherent?
- Are autonomy rules calibrated to risk?
- Are anti-patterns concrete?
- Are environment facts and procedures excluded?
- Is it concise enough to remain salient in the prompt?
- Is user approval explicitly required before installation?
